Start here

Park the ticker. Walk out with cash. Isolated books. Ranked LTVs. Revenue buys the meme.

LOAN MEME is a memecoin that owns a lending protocol. Not a protocol that rented a mascot.

You hold a meme and you do not want to sell it. You post it as collateral, borrow stables against it, and take it back when you repay. These docs say what that costs and how it ends, before you post anything.

Nothing is deployed yet. No contracts, no markets, no token address.

What it is

A lending market for memecoins, isolated by design. Every ticker gets its own book: its own lenders, its own borrow cap, its own LTV ceiling. If one meme goes to zero, that book takes the damage and the book next to it keeps running.

The Yeti is the face. The lending protocol is the product: it holds the collateral, sets the LTV and runs the liquidations. The claim is one line: the first serious meme that runs a meme-lending market.

Two sides

Lender
  • You put in stables and earn the interest borrowers pay.
  • You pick which books you are exposed to. See Lenders.
  • You can lose money. Junior takes the first loss.
Borrower
  • You put in the meme and take out stables.
  • Your ceiling is the LTV of that ticker's league. See Leagues and rank.
  • You pay interest until you repay. If the meme dumps far enough, you are liquidated.

Who should stay out

Any one of these is a reason not to be here

You need the meme back on a fixed date. You cannot survive being liquidated at a bad price. You are borrowing to buy more of the same meme you posted. You are lending money you need next month. Read Risks first.

How a loan works

Deposit a listed meme. Borrow stables against it, up to what its league allows. Interest runs while the loan is open. Repay and get the meme back.

The four steps

One position, start to finish. Every book has two sides: lenders put stables in, you put the meme in and take stables out. Steps 1 and 2 happen in the same transaction. Step 3 runs on its own until you do step 4.

  1. 01Depositmeme in

    You send a listed meme into its own book. It stays locked there. It is not lent out, not staked and not used to farm anything.

    You sendThe meme
    You getA borrow limit
  2. 02Borrowstables out

    The book values your meme at its TWAP: the average price in its main pool over a time window, not the last trade. You can borrow up to a set share of that value, the max LTV of the ticker's league. Borrow less and the meme has room to drop.

    Your limitMeme value × league max LTV
    You getUSDG or the chain stable
  3. 03Pay interestdebt grows

    Interest is added to your debt as time passes, at that book's borrow rate. Most of it goes to the lenders who funded the book. Nothing is due on a schedule. The debt just grows, and the loan drifts toward liquidation even at a flat price.

    RateVariable, per book
    Due dateNone
  4. 04Repaymeme back

    Pay back the stables plus interest and the meme unlocks. You can also repay part of it at any time, which pushes liquidation further away.

    You sendStables + interest
    You getThe meme back
There is no repayment date

The loan stays open as long as it stays healthy. What ends it early is price and interest, not a calendar. See Liquidations.

Look at row (e). Borrow the full 4,500 USDG and a drop of about 22% liquidates you. Borrow 3,000 and it takes about 48%. On a memecoin, that is the difference between surviving a normal Tuesday and not. And interest eats into that gap for as long as the loan stays open.

Health factor

One number tells you how close you are to liquidation. At 1 or above you are fine. Below 1, anyone can liquidate you.

health factor = (collateral value × liquidation threshold) / debt

HF ≥ 1  →  fine
HF < 1  →  liquidatable
  • Collateral value uses the TWAP, not the last trade. A one-block wick does not liquidate you, and it does not save you either.
  • Your debt grows with interest, so a loan you never touch drifts toward liquidation even at a flat price.
  • The liquidation threshold comes from the league. If the ticker drops a league, the tighter threshold applies to your open loan once the change takes effect. Notice period TBD
  • You raise HF two ways: repay some debt, or add more of the same meme.

What you cannot do

  • Borrow memes. Memes only go in as collateral. What comes out is stables.
  • Borrow against a ticker that is not listed. See listing checklist.
  • Borrow 70% against a two-day-old ticker. New tickers start in T3, the lowest limit. A good wallet score adds +5 LTV points at most, never +20.
  • Get a higher LTV because of who is promoting the token. Rank is set on the token. See Leagues.
  • Open a new borrow while the ticker's pool is too thin. That book pauses new borrows until the pool recovers. Repaying still works. See the pause rule.
  • Use MEME_A to back a borrow in the MEME_B book. Every book stands alone.

The risk, on this page

You can lose the deposited meme

If the price falls far enough, or interest piles up, anyone can liquidate you. They repay part of your debt and take that much of your meme, plus a 10–15% bonus TBD. You keep the stables you borrowed. The meme they took does not come back, even if the price recovers an hour later. There is no warning, no grace period and no appeal. Full list on Risks.

Isolated markets

One market is one meme plus one loan asset. MEME_A / USDG dying does not touch MEME_B / USDG. One dump must not brick the whole protocol.

Why this is the whole design

In a shared pool, every lender's stables back every loan. That can work when the collateral is a large, liquid coin. It fails with memecoins: one ticker goes to zero, its loans leave bad debt (debt the collateral can no longer cover), and every lender in the pool pays for it.

Memes go to zero all the time. So we do not pool them. Each listed meme gets its own book: lenders put stables into that book, borrowers put that meme in, and if it goes wrong, the loss stays in that book.

The rules that make it hold

One meme per bookA market is exactly one meme and one loan asset, USDG or the chain stable. No baskets, no "similar tickers" grouped together.
No mixing collateralMEME_A posted in MEME_A / USDG gives you nothing to borrow in MEME_B / USDG. Two positions are two positions.
Losses stay localIf a book ends up with bad debt, the loss lands on the lenders in that book. It is never spread across every lender in the protocol.
A cap per bookEvery book has its own borrow cap, sized to the real liquidity in its main pool. Small for new tickers.
A pause per bookA book can pause new borrows on its own. An empty pool or a stale price on MEME_A does not stop MEME_B.

What isolation costs you

Honest trade-offs, because there are some.

  • No combined limit. You cannot add several memes together into one borrow limit. Each position stands alone.
  • Less liquidity per book. Lenders are spread across books instead of one deep pool, so a given book can run out of stables to borrow.
  • More positions to watch. Three memes means three loans and three health factors, not one.
  • Slower listings. Every new meme is a new book with its own limits, not a line added to a list.
We take all four. The alternative is one dead meme taking the whole protocol with it.
Isolation protects the other books, not yours

If you lend into the book that dies, you take that book's loss. If you borrow in it, you can still be liquidated. Isolation only means the damage stops at the edge of that book. See Risks.

Never ship

A shared pool. Not as a "v2", not as an opt-in, not for T1 only. If it exists, one ticker can eventually reach every lender, and the reason to use this protocol is gone.

Leagues and rank

Your LTV is ranked on the token first, not on the influencer promoting it.

LTV is the share of your meme's value you can borrow. Every listed meme sits in a league, and the league sets two numbers for its book: the max LTV, and the liquidation threshold, the level where your loan can be liquidated. A two-day-old ticker with a thin pool does not get the terms of one that has survived a year with real depth.

A higher league means better terms, not a safer meme. T1 tickers dump too.

What moves a ticker up

  • Age. Time on chain without an incident. There is no shortcut.
  • Depth in its main pool. Enough liquidity that liquidating the book's full borrow cap does not crash the price.
  • Real volume, held over time. Not one day of fake trades.
  • LP locked, verifiably, for a meaningful term.
  • Mint and freeze authority revoked. Required to be listed at all. Nothing moves up without it.

What never moves LTV

Does not count
  • Tweets, follower counts, engagement.
  • "We are partners with…"
  • Team or treasury allocation size.
  • A listing on a centralised venue.
  • Paying us. There is no paid tier.
  • Being a friend of the protocol.
Counts
  • Pool depth you can measure on chain.
  • Days since deploy.
  • Realised volume over a window.
  • Locked LP, with the lock verifiable.
  • Authorities revoked.
  • Survival through a real drawdown.

What moves a ticker down

Rank moves both ways. A ticker whose pool thins out, whose volume dies, or whose LP lock expires drops a league.

  • A demotion lowers max LTV for new borrows straight away.
  • Open loans are not closed by the demotion itself, but the tighter liquidation threshold applies to them once it takes effect. A loan that was safe can become liquidatable. Notice period TBD
  • Demotions are published with the on-chain reason, the same way promotions are.
Never ship

A discretionary whitelist that overrides rank. The moment someone can hand a ticker T1 terms because of who asked, the league table is decoration and lenders are being lied to.

Lenders

You put stables in. Borrowers put memes in and pay you interest. You can lose money, junior most of all, and this page says how.

Where your USDG goes

You deposit the loan asset: USDG or the chain stable, depending on the deployment. It sits in a book and is lent to borrowers who have locked that book's meme against it. Your return is the interest they pay, minus the protocol's share.

You are only exposed to the books you lend into. A book you are not in cannot cost you anything.

You can lose money in junior

That is what junior is. If a T2 or T3 book ends up with bad debt after liquidation, junior lenders take that loss. The higher rate is the payment for it. If you are not comfortable being first in line on thin memecoin books, lend in senior or somewhere else.

Utilisation and getting your money out

Utilisation is the share of a book's stables that is borrowed right now. It sets the borrow rate (the curve is on Fees) and it decides whether you can withdraw right now.

  • Low utilisation. Plenty of free stables, you can withdraw, your rate is low.
  • High utilisation. Rates rise, which pays you more and pushes borrowers to repay.
  • At 100%. There is nothing free to withdraw. You wait for a repayment or for new deposits. The book is not broke and nothing is bugged, but your money is stuck until then.

Oracle and listing

Price comes from a TWAP of the canonical pool, not the last tick. If the pool is empty, new borrows pause. A ticker only gets a book if it passes every listing check.

Why TWAP, not last tick

The last tick is the price of the last trade in the pool. On a memecoin it is cheap to move. If collateral were priced off it, anyone could pump the price, borrow against the fake value and walk, or dump it and liquidate someone else for the bonus.

So every book prices its meme with a TWAP, a time-weighted average price: the average price in the pool over a set window. To move a TWAP you have to hold the price away from the market for the whole window, which costs real money and gives everyone else time to trade against you.

SourceThe canonical pool: the one main pool for that token on the chain where the book lives, named at listing.
MethodTime-weighted average price over a fixed window. Window length TBD
Not usedThe last tick. Prices from other pools or exchanges. Any price we cannot rebuild from chain data.
Trade-offA TWAP lags. In a fast dump it shows a price higher than the real one, which delays liquidation and makes bad debt more likely. That is the cost of a price that is expensive to manipulate, and it is one reason the LTVs are low.

The pause rule

Empty pool, no new borrows

If the canonical pool's liquidity falls below the book's minimum, or the price stops updating, that book pauses new borrows. Repaying still works. Liquidations still work. Only new debt is blocked, and only in that book.

How the pause behaves

  • The pause is per book. Other markets keep running.
  • It is a rule, not a judgement call. The minimum is set at listing and published.
  • Borrowing resumes on its own when the pool recovers. How far above the minimum: TBD
  • A paused book can still liquidate into a thin pool, so a pause is a warning sign for that book's lenders, not protection.

Listing checklist

A ticker gets a book only if every line is true. Not most of them.

Mint authority revoked. If someone can still print more supply, we do not list it. No exceptions.
Freeze authority revoked. A meme someone else can freeze is not collateral.
A canonical pool named at listing, deep enough to absorb a liquidation of the book's full borrow cap.
Minimum liquidity in that pool, measured over a window, not at a single moment. TBD
Minimum age for the league it enters. TBD
A normal token. No balances that change on their own, no tax on transfers, nothing that can block a transfer during a liquidation.
A borrow cap set from the pool's real depth, low at first.

Listed means the meme can be used as collateral. It does not mean the meme is safe to hold.

Liquidations and bad debt

When your health factor drops below 1, anyone can repay part of your debt and take that much of your meme, plus a bonus. If the meme gaps down too fast for that, the loss stays in that book.

When it happens

Your health factor (HF) compares what your meme is worth, cut down by the league's liquidation threshold, with what you owe.

health factor = (collateral value × liquidation threshold) / debt

HF < 1  →  the position can be liquidated by anyone

Two things push you there:

  • The meme falls. Collateral value drops, measured on the TWAP.
  • Interest adds up. Debt grows on its own. A loan that is barely healthy and never touched will cross below 1, even at a flat price.

The liquidation bonus

A liquidator repays part of your debt and receives that much of your meme, plus a bonus. The bonus is the only reason anyone shows up.

Range10–15%. Per-league values TBD
Why not 5%Thin meme books do not clear at a 5% tip. The liquidator has to sell the meme they receive, and in a thin pool that sale moves the price more than 5%. They would lose money, so nobody bids, and the loan rots into bad debt.
Who pays itThe borrower, out of the meme they posted. It is the cost of being liquidated.
Protocol shareA slice of the bonus may be retained as protocol revenue. TBD. See Fees.

Pre-liquidation

The plan is a softer path, if we can ship it: as a loan gets close to HF 1, part of it is closed early at a smaller penalty, instead of waiting for a full liquidation at the full bonus. Better for the borrower, and better for the book, because it sells into liquidity that still exists.

TBD, ships only if we can build it safely. If it does not ship, liquidation is the ordinary path and this page says so.

Bad debt

Bad debt is what is left when the meme runs out before the debt does. It happens when the price gaps down faster than liquidators can act, which, on a memecoin, is a normal Tuesday, not a rare event.

The shortfall stays in that book

It is not spread across the protocol. The lenders in that book take it. With the two vaults, that means junior for a T2 or T3 book and senior for a T1 book. Lenders in other books lose nothing. This is the whole reason the markets are isolated.

What that means in practice

  • There is no insurance that always pays. A reserve may cover part of a small shortfall. It is not a guarantee and we will never describe it as one.
  • Any bad-debt event is published in full: the book, the loan, the price path, the size, and what the reserve did or did not cover.
  • Buybacks pause while a book has bad debt. Covering the loss comes before buying the token. Trigger TBD

How not to get liquidated

  • Borrow well under the max. The gap between what you borrowed and the liquidation threshold is your whole buffer.
  • Watch the debt, not just the price. Interest moves your HF on a flat chart.
  • Repay early. Partial repayments work and count straight away.
  • Watch the league. A demotion tightens the threshold on your open loan. See Leagues.
  • Do not borrow to buy more of the meme you posted. When it drops, you lose on the extra meme and get liquidated sooner on the loan.

Fees

Borrowers pay interest. Most of it goes to lenders. The protocol's slice funds the reserve and buys back LOAN MEME.

Borrow rate shape

The borrow rate depends on utilisation: the share of a book's stables that is borrowed right now. Up to a point, called the kink, the rate stays low so borrowing is worth doing. Past the kink it climbs hard, which pays lenders enough to bring new stables in and pushes borrowers to repay before the book runs dry.

The shape is settled. The slopes, where the kink sits and the rate at 100% are set per league and are TBD, which is why the chart above has no values on either axis.

Both rates move. A borrower can see the rate spike when a book fills up. A lender can see it fall to almost nothing when borrowers repay.

The LOAN MEME token

The meme is the culture. The protocol is the engine. Protocol revenue buys back the meme. You do not need to hold it to lend or borrow.

What the token does

It does
  • Carry the brand. It is the thing people hold and post, with the Yeti as its face.
  • Get bought on the open market with protocol fees.
  • Stay out of the lending books. It is not collateral, so no book can liquidate it.
  • Meet the same standard as every listed meme: mint and freeze revoked, contract verified, wallets published.
It does not
  • Gate anything. You lend and borrow without holding it.
  • Work as collateral in the protocol at launch.
  • Pay yield, dividends or any other payout.
  • Make you an owner. It is not equity, not a claim on assets, and not a promise of any kind.
It benefits through buybacks, or not at all. Forcing the token into borrowing would make the markets worse and the fees smaller.

Risks

Read this before you deposit anything. If a risk here is missing, the page is wrong, tell us.

If you borrow

LiquidationIf the TWAP falls far enough, anyone can repay part of your debt and take that much of your meme, plus a 10–15% bonus TBD. No warning, no grace period, no appeal.
Zero versus your entryThe meme can go to zero, and your loss counts from where you bought, not from where you borrowed. Say you bought at $0.04 and got liquidated at $0.005: the loan does not undo that first loss.
Illiquid exitThe TWAP is not the price you get. Selling a meme into a thin pool pushes the price down as you sell, so you can get far less than the value the book shows.
Interest driftYour debt grows all the time. A loan you never touch moves toward liquidation on a flat chart.
Rate spikesBorrow rates rise sharply when a book fills up. What you pay is not fixed.
League changeIf your ticker is demoted, the liquidation threshold tightens on the loan you already have. A safe loan can become liquidatable without the price moving. See Leagues.
Borrowing to buy moreBorrowing stables to buy more of the meme you posted doubles your bet on one price. It is the fastest way to lose the position.

If you lend

Bad debtA meme can gap below the point where liquidation covers the debt. The loss lands on the lenders in that book, and junior holds the new and thin books, where memes die first. You can lose part of your deposit.
No guaranteed backstopA reserve may cover part of a small shortfall. There is no insurance that always pays, and we will not describe one.
Illiquid exitWhen all of a book's stables are borrowed, there is nothing free to withdraw. You wait for repayments or new deposits. Your money is not there on demand.
Rate collapseBorrowers repay, fewer stables are borrowed, and you earn close to nothing while your money sits.
Stablecoin riskYou hold the loan asset. If USDG or the chain stable loses its $1 peg, that is your loss, and nothing in this protocol offsets it.

Risks either way

OracleA TWAP lags. In a fast dump it shows a price above the real one, which delays liquidation and makes bad debt more likely. A TWAP can still be manipulated. It is expensive to do, which is not the same as impossible.
Empty poolIf the canonical pool thins out, new borrows pause in that book. Liquidations still fire, into liquidity that may not be there.
Smart contract failureA bug in the market, the locker that holds the memes, the price feed or the fee router can take everything, in any league, in any vault.
Chain and bridge riskYou take on the risk of the chain a book lives on: outages, halts, bugs in the chain itself. A bridged token carries the bridge's risk on top of its own.
Key and governance riskWhoever holds the keys can change parameters. Who they are, how many must sign and how long a change waits all go on Contracts. Read them and decide whether you accept them.
App riskAn app is a window onto the contracts, and it can be wrong. If the screen and the chain disagree, the chain is right.
Pre-deploy riskNothing is live. Any address, app or "presale" claiming to be this protocol today is not.

FAQ

Short answers. Longer ones are linked.

Is this just a meme?

Yes, plus a market. The meme is the culture: the brand, the token, the Yeti. The market is isolated lending against memecoins, and it is where the fees come from. Protocol revenue buys back the meme.

Can I borrow 70% against a two-day-old ticker?

No. A new ticker with a thin book is T3, around 30% max LTV TBD. 70% is not a day-one number for anything here. See Leagues and rank.

If my meme goes to zero, does every lender eat the loss?

No. That market. Only the lenders in that book take the loss. Lenders in other books lose nothing. That is what isolated means. See Isolated markets.

Is the Yeti the protocol?

No. The Yeti is the face. It is in the art and the videos. It holds no key, has no vote and appears in no formula. The protocol is the lending market.

Can I lose money?

Yes. Borrowers can be liquidated and lose the meme they posted. Lenders can lose part of their deposit when a book ends up with bad debt, and junior is first in line. The token can go to zero. See Risks.

What do I actually deposit, and what do I get out?

Borrowers put the meme in and take stables out, USDG or the chain stable. Lenders put stables in and earn the interest. Memes are never lent out. They go in as collateral only.

What price is used to liquidate me?

A TWAP of the canonical pool, not the last tick: the average price in the meme's main pool over a set window. One wick does not liquidate you. It also does not save you if the price stays down. See Oracle and listing.

Do I need to hold LOAN MEME to lend or borrow?

No. The token gates nothing. It receives buybacks funded by protocol fees; it is not a toll on using the market.

Can I use LOAN MEME as collateral?

Not at launch. A protocol accepting its own token as collateral in its own market is the standard way this category blows up.

How does a ticker get listed?

It passes the whole checklist or it does not get a book: mint and freeze revoked, a canonical pool with minimum liquidity, minimum age for its league. All of it, not most of it. See the listing checklist.

Can I pay to get a higher LTV?

No. There is no paid tier and no whitelist that overrides rank. Rank comes from things you can measure on the token. A borrower's wallet score adds +0 to +5 LTV points. Never +20.

Which chains are live?

None. Memes from Robinhood Chain, Base and BSC can live in the LOAN MEME universe, but a market only exists where we have deployed, and we have not deployed anywhere. The chain table on Oracle and listing stays empty until that changes.

What is the APY?

There isn't one, because there is no live market. Once markets exist, we publish the rates they actually paid over a stated period. No projections, and no yearly number stretched out of one good week.

Are you affiliated with Robinhood, or any exchange?

No. Not official, not endorsed, not partnered. Tokens that originate on any chain may end up listed as collateral; that is not a relationship with anyone.

Is there a token address yet?

No. Anything circulating now is not ours. Addresses will only ever appear on Contracts.

Roadmap of the mechanism

What gets built, in what order, and what does not get built at all.

Phases

PhaseWhat shipsDone when
T0 First isolated markets. Tiny caps. A few tickers, on conservative terms. Real borrows, real repayments, and one full liquidation cycle observed on a live book.
T1 Leagues live. Public listing rules, applied the same way to every ticker. A ticker gets promoted and another gets demoted, both on published criteria.
T2 Buyback routing on. Fee split frozen and published. Every transfer and purchase checkable on chain. Fees earned on live markets have bought the token on the open market, with hashes.
T3 More chains. Only after the first chain has survived a real dump, not before.
Current phase: pre-T0, nothing deployed

Phases do not have dates. A phase ends when its condition is met, and stating a date we cannot control is the same as making it up.

Never ship

Three things that are off the table permanently

A shared pool. Not as a v2, not opt-in, not for T1 only.
70% LTV on day one. Not for any league, for any ticker, for anyone.
A discretionary whitelist that overrides rank. If someone can hand out terms by hand, the league table is decoration.

These are not "current thinking". They are the constraints the protocol is for. If any of them ships, the reason to use this instead of a generic lending market is gone.

Wanted, not promised

Anything in this list that has not shipped is described in these docs as not shipped. It never gets written as if it exists.

  • Pre-liquidation. A partial close before full liquidation, at a smaller penalty. Ships only if we can build it safely. See Liquidations.
  • Senior and junior vaults. Senior for T1 books, junior for T2 and T3. Ships only if we can build it right. See Lenders.
  • Wallet score. +0 to +5 LTV points when it arrives. Never +20.
  • A public dashboard for fees earned and tokens bought, so the buyback claim can be checked, not just stated.
