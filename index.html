Whether you are holding your first meme or you have been rotating tickers for years, these docs exist to tell you exactly what happens to your bag when you post it as collateral — before you post it.

What it is

Loan Meme is a lending market for memecoins, and it is isolated by design.

You hold a meme. You do not want to sell it. You deposit it into that meme's market and borrow stables against it. Later you repay the stables plus interest and take your meme back. On the other side, lenders put stables in and earn the interest borrowers pay.

Every meme gets its own book. What varies on the position is the LTV, depending on that book's liquidity, volatility and how it is trading. A riskier meme gets its LTV cut, so one wick doesn't wipe the vault.

Two sides, two things you put in

Lender
  • You put in stables.
  • You earn interest paid by borrowers.
  • You choose which books you are exposed to. See Lenders.
  • You can lose money. Junior takes the first loss.
Borrower
  • You put in the meme.
  • You take out stables, up to the LTV your ticker's league allows.
  • You pay interest until you repay.
  • If the meme dumps far enough, you get liquidated.

What it is not

  • Not a shared pool. There is no single pot where one bad ticker drains everyone.
  • Not a place to lend your meme out. Memes are collateral, never the withdrawal asset.
  • Not a bank, not equity, not affiliated with any exchange or chain operator.
  • Not a way to borrow 70% against a two-day-old ticker. See Leagues and rank.
  • Not deployed. Nothing on this site is live yet.

The Yeti is the face, not the protocol

The ice-blue Yeti is the brand. It is in the art and the videos. It has no role in the contracts, no vote, no key, and it does not appear in any formula in these docs. If you came from the videos: the window the Yeti walks up to is a market, the bag is your meme, the ice brick is the loan. That is the only crossover.

Who should stay out

Do not use this if any of these are true

You need the deposited meme back on a fixed date. You cannot survive being liquidated at a bad price. You are borrowing to buy more of the same meme you posted. You do not know what a TWAP is and have not read Risks. You are lending money you need next month.

How a loan works

Deposit a listed meme. Borrow stables against it. Pay interest. Repay and take the meme back.

The four steps

One position, start to finish. Steps 1 and 2 happen in the same transaction. Step 3 runs on its own until you choose to do step 4.

  1. 01Depositmeme in

    You send a listed meme into its market. It is locked as collateral — not lent to anyone, not staked anywhere, not rehypothecated.

    You sendThe meme
    You getBorrowing power
  2. 02Borrowstables out

    The market prices your collateral off the TWAP of its canonical pool, then applies the max LTV for that ticker's league. You draw stables up to that limit — usually less, if you want room to breathe.

    Limit set byTWAP × league LTV
    You getStables
  3. 03Pay interestdebt grows

    Interest accrues on what you borrowed, per block, at the market's borrow rate. Nothing is due on a schedule — it just grows your debt, and your health factor falls with it.

    ChargedPer block, variable rate
    Due dateNone
  4. 04Repaymeme back

    You repay the stables plus accrued interest and your meme is unlocked. Partial repayments work and improve your health factor immediately.

    You sendStables + interest
    You getThe meme back
There is no repayment date

The loan stays open as long as your position is healthy. What ends it early is price, not time — see Liquidations.

Health factor

health factor = (collateral value × liquidation threshold) / debt

HF ≥ 1  →  fine
HF < 1  →  liquidatable
  • Collateral value uses the TWAP, not the last tick. A one-block wick does not liquidate you, and it does not save you either.
  • Your debt grows with interest, so a position left alone drifts toward liquidation even at a flat price.
  • You improve HF two ways: repay debt, or add collateral.

What you cannot do

  • Borrow the meme. Memes go in as collateral only. The withdrawal asset is stables.
  • Borrow against a ticker that is not listed. See listing checklist.
  • Get a higher LTV because of who is promoting the token. Rank is set on the token. See Leagues.
  • Use collateral in one market to back a borrow in another. Books are isolated.

The risk, on this page

You can lose the deposited meme

If the price falls far enough, a liquidator repays part of your debt and takes your collateral at a discount. You keep the stables you borrowed. You do not keep the meme. There is no warning, no grace period and no appeal. Full list on Risks.

Leagues and rank

Your LTV comes from the token, not from the person promoting it.

Every listed meme sits in a league. The league sets the max LTV and the liquidation threshold for that book. A ticker with a two-day-old chart and a thin pool does not get the same terms as one that has survived a year and has real depth. That is the entire idea.

What moves a ticker up

  • Age. Time on chain without an incident. There is no substitute and no way to accelerate it.
  • Depth in the canonical pool. Enough liquidity that a full liquidation at the book's borrow cap clears without eating the bonus.
  • Sustained real volume, not a single day of wash prints.
  • LP locked, verifiably, for a meaningful term.
  • Mint and freeze authority revoked. Required for T1, and required for listing at all.

What never moves a ticker

Does not count
  • Tweets, follower counts, engagement.
  • "We are partners with…"
  • Team or treasury allocation size.
  • A listing on a centralised venue.
  • Paying us. There is no paid tier.
  • Being a friend of the protocol.
Counts
  • Pool depth you can measure on chain.
  • Days since deploy.
  • Realised volume over a window.
  • Locked LP, with the lock verifiable.
  • Authorities revoked.
  • Survival through a real drawdown.

Demotion

Rank moves both ways. A ticker whose pool thins out, whose volume dies, or whose LP lock expires drops a league.

  • A demotion lowers max LTV for new borrows immediately.
  • Existing positions are not force-closed by the demotion itself, but the tighter liquidation threshold applies once it takes effect. Notice period TBD
  • Demotions are published with the on-chain reason, the same way promotions are.
Never ship

A discretionary whitelist that overrides rank. The moment someone can hand a ticker T1 terms because of who asked, the league table is decoration and lenders are being lied to.

Lenders

You put stables in. Borrowers pay you interest. You can lose money — this page says exactly how.

Where your stables go

You deposit the loan asset — USDG or the chain stable, depending on the deployment. It sits in a book and is lent to borrowers who have locked a meme against it. Your return is the interest those borrowers pay, minus the protocol's share.

You are never exposed to a meme you did not choose exposure to. A book you are not in cannot cost you anything.

Junior can lose principal

That is what junior is. If a T3 book ends up with bad debt after liquidation, junior absorbs it before senior sees anything. A higher rate is the payment for that. If you are not comfortable being the first loss on a thin memecoin book, be in senior or be somewhere else.

Utilisation and getting your money out

Utilisation is the share of a book's stables that is currently borrowed. It sets the borrow rate — the curve is on Fees — and it decides whether you can withdraw right now.

  • Low utilisation. Plenty of free liquidity, you can withdraw, your rate is low.
  • High utilisation. Rates rise, which pays you more and pushes borrowers to repay.
  • At 100%. There is nothing free to withdraw. You wait for a repayment or for new deposits. This is not a bug and it is not insolvency — but it is a real constraint on your money.

Oracle and listing

Price comes from a TWAP of the canonical pool. If the pool is empty, new borrows stop.

Why TWAP, not last tick

A memecoin's last trade is trivially cheap to move. If collateral were priced off the last tick, anyone could push the price up, borrow against the inflated value and walk, or push it down and liquidate someone else's position for the bonus.

So every market prices its collateral as a time-weighted average over a window. Moving a TWAP means holding the price away from the market for the whole window, which costs real money and gives everyone else time to trade against you.

SourceThe canonical pool for that token on the deployment chain. One pool, declared at listing.
MethodTime-weighted average price over a fixed window. Window length TBD
Not usedLast tick. Spot. Aggregated off-chain quotes. Any feed we cannot reconstruct from chain data.
Trade-offA TWAP lags. In a fast dump it reports a price higher than reality, which delays liquidation and makes bad debt more likely. That is the cost of not being manipulable, and it is priced into the LTVs.

The pause rule

Empty pool, no new borrows

If the canonical pool's liquidity falls below the book's floor, or the feed goes stale, that book stops issuing new borrows. Repayments still work. Liquidations still work. Only new debt is blocked, and only in that book.

How the pause behaves

  • The pause is per book. Other markets are unaffected.
  • It is a rule, not a judgement call — the threshold is set at listing and published.
  • Borrowing resumes automatically when the pool recovers above the floor. Hysteresis TBD

Listing checklist

A ticker gets a market only if every line is true. Not most of them.

Mint authority revoked. If someone can print more supply, we do not list it. No exceptions.
Freeze authority revoked. Collateral that a third party can freeze is not collateral.
A canonical pool exists and is nominated at listing, with enough depth to clear a liquidation at the book's borrow cap.
Minimum liquidity in that pool, measured over a window, not at a single moment.
Minimum age for the league being applied for.
Standard token behaviour. No rebasing, no fee on transfer, no transfer hooks that can block a liquidation.
A borrow cap set from the pool's real depth, low at first.

Liquidations and bad debt

When health factor drops below 1, someone else repays your debt and takes your collateral at a discount.

When it happens

health factor = (collateral value × liquidation threshold) / debt

HF < 1  →  the position can be liquidated by anyone

Two things push you there:

  • The meme falls. Collateral value drops, measured on the TWAP.
  • Interest accrues. Debt grows on its own. A position at HF 1.05 that you never touch will eventually cross, even at a flat price.

The liquidation bonus

A liquidator repays part of your debt and receives your collateral at a discount. That discount is the bonus, and it is the only reason anyone shows up.

Range10–15%. Per-league values TBD
Why not 5%A 5% tip works on deep blue-chip collateral. On a thin meme book, the liquidator's own sell moves the price more than 5%, so they lose money and simply do not bid — and the position rots into bad debt.
Who pays itThe borrower, out of collateral. It is the cost of having been liquidated.
Protocol shareA slice of the bonus may be retained as protocol revenue. TBD — see Fees.

Pre-liquidation

The intent is to ship a softer path: as a position approaches HF 1, it can be partially unwound early at a smaller penalty instead of waiting for a full liquidation at the full bonus. Better for the borrower, and better for the book, because it sells into liquidity that still exists.

TBD — ships only if we can build it safely. If it does not ship, liquidation is the ordinary path and this page says so.

What a liquidation looks like

  1. TWAP updates. Collateral value falls, HF crosses below 1.
  2. A liquidator calls the market. Anyone can. It is permissionless and there is no queue.
  3. They repay up to the close factor of your debt in stables.
  4. They take collateral equal to what they repaid plus the bonus.
  5. Position updates. If HF is back above 1 it stops. If not, it can be liquidated again.
  6. You keep the borrowed stables. You do not keep the collateral that was taken.

Bad debt

Bad debt is what is left when collateral runs out before the debt does. It happens when price gaps faster than liquidators can act — which, on a memecoin, is a normal Tuesday, not a black swan.

The shortfall stays in that book

It is not socialised across the protocol. Lenders exposed to that market absorb it — junior first, then senior if junior is wiped. Lenders in other books lose nothing. This is the whole reason the markets are isolated.

What that means in practice

  • There is no insurance fund that always pays. A reserve may cover part of a small shortfall. It is not a guarantee and we will never describe it as one.
  • Any bad-debt event is published in full: the book, the position, the price path, the size, and what the reserve did or did not cover.
  • Buyback routing pauses while a book is in shortfall. Solvency before the loop. Trigger TBD

How not to get liquidated

  • Borrow well under the max. The gap between your LTV and the liquidation threshold is your entire buffer.
  • Watch the debt, not just the price. Interest moves your HF on a flat chart.
  • Repay early. Partial repayments work and take effect immediately.
  • Do not borrow to buy more of the collateral you posted. That position liquidates twice as fast in both directions.

Fees

Borrowers pay interest. Most of it goes to lenders. A slice is protocol revenue, and that slice buys the meme.

Borrow rate shape

The borrow rate is a function of utilisation, with a kink. Below the kink the rate stays low so borrowing is worth doing. Above it the rate climbs hard, which pays lenders enough to bring new stables in and pushes borrowers to repay before the book runs dry.

The shape is settled. The slopes, the kink position and the rate at 100% are per-league and TBD, which is why the chart above carries no values on either axis.

The LOAN MEME token

The meme is the culture layer. Protocol revenue buys it back. It is not required to use the protocol.

What the token does

It does
  • Carry the brand. It is the thing people hold and post.
  • Receive protocol fee revenue through open-market buybacks.
  • Stay outside the lending risk. It is not collateral, so no book can liquidate it.
  • Hold itself to the listing standard: authorities revoked, contract verified, wallets published.
It does not
  • Gate anything. You lend and borrow without holding it.
  • Act as collateral in the protocol at launch.
  • Pay a yield, a dividend or a distribution.
  • Represent equity, a claim on assets, or a promise of any kind.
It captures value on the buyback side or not at all. Forcing the token into the borrow flow would make the markets worse and the revenue smaller.

Buyback source

  1. Fees accrue in stables inside each book — the interest slice, the liquidation bonus slice, and a listing fee if one exists.
  2. Reserve is served first. A book in shortfall does not route to buyback.
  3. The remainder is swept on a schedule to the buyback route.
  4. It buys LOAN MEME on the open market. No OTC, no private fills.
  5. Every step is published with transaction hashes.
Only earned money buys the token

Never treasury sales, never borrowed funds, never new emissions relabelled as revenue. Small revenue means a small buyback, and we publish the small number rather than dressing it up.

What happens to the tokens once bought — burn or hold — is TBD, and gets decided before routing goes live.

Risks

FAQ

Short answers. Longer ones are linked.

Is this just a meme?

It is a meme, plus a market. The meme is the brand and the token. The market is real isolated lending against memecoins, and it is where the fees come from. Both are true at once, and we do not pretend either one away.

Can I borrow 70% against a two-day-old ticker?

No. A new ticker with a thin book is T3, which is around 30% max LTV. 70% is not a day-one number for anything here. See Leagues and rank.

If my meme rugs, does every lender eat it?

No. That market's lenders do — junior before senior. Lenders in other books lose nothing. That is what isolated means, and it is the reason the protocol is built this way.

Is the Yeti the protocol?

No. The Yeti is the face. It is in the art and the videos. It holds no key, has no vote and appears in no formula. The protocol is the lending market.

What do I actually deposit, and what do I get out?

Borrowers deposit the meme and take out stables. Lenders deposit stables and earn interest. Memes are never lent out — they go in as collateral only.

What price is used to liquidate me?

A TWAP of the canonical pool, not the last tick. One wick does not liquidate you. It also does not save you if the price genuinely stays down. See Oracle and listing.

Do I need to hold LOAN MEME to lend or borrow?

No. The token gates nothing. It receives buybacks funded by protocol fees; it is not a toll on using the market.

Can I use LOAN MEME as collateral?

Not at launch. A protocol accepting its own token as collateral in its own market is the standard way this category blows up.

How does a ticker get listed?

It has to pass the whole checklist: mint and freeze authority revoked, a nominated canonical pool with minimum liquidity, minimum age for its league, standard token behaviour, and a borrow cap sized to real depth. All of it, not most of it. See the listing checklist.

Can I pay to get a higher LTV?

No. There is no paid tier and no discretionary whitelist. Rank comes from measurable properties of the token. A borrower's wallet history can add up to +5 LTV points, never more.

Which chains are live?

None. Memes from Robinhood Chain, Base and BSC live in the Loan Meme universe, but a market only exists where we have deployed, and we have not deployed. The chain table on Oracle and listing stays empty until that changes.

What is the APY?

There isn't one, because there is no live market. We publish realised rates for a stated period once markets exist. We do not publish projections, and we do not annualise a good week.

Are you affiliated with Robinhood, or any exchange?

No. Not official, not endorsed, not partnered. Tokens that originate on any chain may end up listed as collateral; that is not a relationship with anyone.

Is there a token address yet?

No. Anything circulating now is not ours. Addresses will only ever appear on Contracts.

Roadmap of the mechanism

What gets built, in what order, and what does not get built at all.

Phases

PhaseWhat shipsDone when
T0 First isolated markets. Tiny caps. A handful of tickers. Manual, conservative parameters. Real borrows, real repayments, and one full liquidation cycle observed on a live book.
T1 Leagues live. Listing rules published and applied mechanically rather than by hand. A ticker gets promoted and another gets demoted, both on published criteria.
T2 Buyback routing on. Fee split frozen and published. Every sweep and purchase verifiable. Fees earned on live markets have bought the token on the open market, with hashes.
T3 More chains. Only after the first chain has survived a real dump — not before.
Current phase: pre-T0 nothing deployed

Phases do not have dates. A phase ends when its condition is met, and stating a date we cannot control is the same as making it up.

Never ship

Three things that are off the table permanently

A shared pool. Not as a v2, not opt-in, not for T1 only.
70% LTV on day one. Not for any league, for any ticker, for anyone.
A discretionary whitelist that overrides rank. If someone can hand out terms by hand, the league table is decoration.

These are not "current thinking". They are the constraints the protocol is for. If any of them ships, the reason to use this instead of a generic lending market is gone.

Wanted, not promised

Anything in this list that has not shipped is described in these docs as not shipped. It never gets written as if it exists.

  • Pre-liquidation. A softer unwind before full liquidation. Ships only if it can be built safely. See Liquidations.
  • Senior and junior vaults. Ships only if the loss waterfall is airtight. See Lenders.
  • Wallet score. Capped at +5 LTV points forever, whenever it arrives.
  • A public dashboard for fees earned, tokens bought and float, so the buyback claim is checkable rather than asserted.
